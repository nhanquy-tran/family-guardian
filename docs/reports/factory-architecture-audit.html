Factory architecture audit

The second full critical review, after the factory built version 0.2/0.3 on its own. It draws on 217 metered agent runs ($55.4), 1,000 workflow runs, 79 merged PRs and every gate comment since the v4 upgrade (ADR 0017) and light mode (ADR 0018). It covers: what the logs say about efficiency and quality, how well the factory fits greenfield and brownfield projects up to 300k LOC, findings by dimension compared with published practice from frontier labs and large engineering companies, and a consolidated, costed plan.

Verdict

The v4 upgrade worked. The factory has a source of truth (29 capability specs), a scheduler that orders work by Depends on and Touches, branch protection (every one of the 35 commits since went through a gated PR), and light mode cut the core pipeline cost to about $1.43 per item. It shipped 8 features in about 5.5 hours of wall time.

Three weaknesses remain, and the logs show them clearly. A third of the spend is rework (fixer and debugger runs, rebuilds). The quality work has moved into the human-level gate: every light-mode spec and half the light-mode builds needed gate findings that no agent raised, and that gate is an unmetered chat session. The factory is still fragile at the edges: 8 factory-caused failures in about 13 hours, a docs job that has never succeeded, and turn caps that don't stop agents. The next gains come from turning the gate's checklist into code and agents, and from cutting wasted turns. More agents won't help.

Fit for projects up to 300k LOC

Family Guardian is about 19k lines of TypeScript and SQL with 737 tests and a 57-second CI. That is the factory's proven envelope. Beyond it, the limits are test time, baseline specs and code navigation, not the agent roles.

Overall: the factory matches reference practice on spec-driven flow, sandboxing, guardrails and orchestration shape, which is where most home-grown factories fall short. It lags on the three things large teams invest in once the loop works: autonomous gating, user-level verification, and evals for the factory itself.

Recommendation: do R1–R5 now, then R6 and R7 together, then R8.

  • Expected after "Now": cost per item from $2.14 to about $1.60, rework share from 33 % to under 20 %, spec fix rounds at most 3 in 8, mean lead time from 3.1 h to about 2 h.
  • Expected after "Next": the factory merges low-risk items without the chat session; high-risk items cost about $2.00 with full checks; the owner sees only escalations. This is the condition for the brownfield pilot.
  • Do not add: more agent roles, a model gateway, or OpenHands. The logs show no failure those would fix.
  • Budget: about $25 of the $80 limit remains. R1–R5 need about $5 of agent runs to verify; R10 needs about $10 per replay.
